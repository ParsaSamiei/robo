# 12 — CONTENT, COPY & BILINGUAL CONTENT SYSTEM

**Document:** `12_CONTENT_SYSTEM.md`

This document defines how content should be written, stored, translated, presented, and managed across the robotics club website.

The most important principle is:

> **The website should communicate real engineering work clearly, not manufacture impressive-sounding marketing language.**

The team is young, currently in its first year, and still developing its identity. The website should present that honestly while still looking ambitious and professional.

---

# 1. Content Philosophy

The content should feel:

- Confident.
- Technical when appropriate.
- Human.
- Concise.
- Honest.
- Ambitious.
- Evidence-based.

Avoid:

- Corporate jargon.
- Empty claims.
- Excessive superlatives.
- Fake accomplishments.
- AI-generated sounding copy.
- Generic phrases such as "revolutionizing the future of robotics."

---

# 2. Voice

The default voice should be approximately:

```text
70% professional
30% student / human
```

This means the site should sound like engineers who know what they are doing, while still showing that the organization is young and learning.

---

# 3. English First

English is the preferred language.

All primary content should be written in English first.

Persian should be a proper translation/adaptation, not an afterthought.

---

# 4. Persian Is Not a Literal Translation

The Persian version should preserve:

- Meaning.
- Tone.
- Technical accuracy.
- Emotional impact.

But it does not need to follow English sentence structure.

For example, do not produce Persian text that feels like machine-translated English.

---

# 5. Technical Terminology

Technical terms should generally remain recognizable.

Examples:

```text
ROS 2
Python
C++
YOLO
SLAM
LiDAR
Jetson
STM
SolidWorks
Altium Designer
```

Do not unnecessarily translate established technical names.

---

# 6. Acronyms

On first appearance, use the full term where appropriate.

Example:

> Smart Manufacturing League (SML)

After that:

> SML

Likewise:

> Robot Operating System 2 (ROS 2)

if the context requires explanation.

---

# 7. Audience Awareness

Every page should be written for one or more of the main audiences:

```text
Sponsors
Competition organizers
Robotics teams
General public
University
Potential members
```

The copy should not try to speak to all six audiences in every sentence.

---

# 8. Audience Priority

Overall:

```text
1. Potential members
2. Robotics / technical community
3. Competition organizers
4. Sponsors
5. IUST / university
6. General public
```

However, individual pages can prioritize different audiences.

---

# 9. Homepage Audience

The homepage should balance:

```text
Potential members
+
Robotics community
+
Sponsors
```

A visitor should understand the organization even without robotics expertise.

---

# 10. Sponsor Audience

Sponsor-facing content should emphasize:

- Engineering capability.
- Team talent.
- Projects.
- Competition participation.
- Visibility.
- Collaboration.
- Long-term potential.

Do not make unsupported claims about reach or audience size.

---

# 11. Competition Audience

Competition organizers should quickly find:

- Team identity.
- Competition history.
- Robots.
- Results.
- Technical capabilities.
- Contact information.

---

# 12. Potential Member Audience

Potential members need to understand:

```text
What do you build?
What areas exist?
Do I need experience?
What can I learn?
How do I apply?
```

The Join page should answer these questions directly.

---

# 13. General Public

The general public should be able to understand:

> What does this team do?

without knowing:

- ROS 2.
- SLAM.
- LiDAR.
- YOLO.
- Jetson.

Technical pages can go deeper.

---

# 14. Homepage Hero Copy

Do not lock the final hero copy into the technical implementation document.

Instead, the AI implementing the website should produce **multiple copy directions** and select the strongest one.

The final hero should contain:

```text
Identity
+
What the team does
+
Primary CTA
+
Secondary CTA
```

---

# 15. Hero Copy Constraints

The hero should not become a paragraph.

Recommended:

```text
Eyebrow
Short headline
Short supporting sentence
1–2 CTAs
```

---

# 16. Example Copy Direction

This is an example of tone, **not final copy**:

> We build robots to solve real engineering problems.

Supporting:

> A student-led robotics club at IUST, combining mechanical, hardware, and software engineering through competition, research, and hands-on development.

---

# 17. Avoid Empty Hero Claims

Avoid:

> Shaping the future of intelligent automation.

Avoid:

> Revolutionizing industrial robotics.

Avoid:

> Pioneering tomorrow's autonomous world.

Unless the website can substantiate such claims.

---

# 18. About Copy

The About page should tell the story of:

```text
Why the club exists
+
Where it started
+
What it does today
+
Where it wants to go
```

---

# 19. First-Year Positioning

The team started this year.

This should be framed positively.

Recommended positioning:

> We are at the beginning of the journey.

not:

> We are a leading robotics organization.

The website can communicate ambition without pretending to have an established legacy.

---

# 20. First Competition

The current competition is:

> Smart Manufacturing League (SML)

The website should explain its relevance without making the entire identity dependent on it.

---

# 21. Competition Positioning

Suggested conceptual message:

> Our first competitive focus is the Smart Manufacturing League, where we apply robotics engineering to an industrial environment.

This communicates:

- Current focus.
- Engineering.
- Competition.
- Industrial relevance.

---

# 22. Club vs Competition

This distinction is critical.

The website represents:

> **A robotics club.**

The club currently participates in:

> **SML.**

Therefore:

```text
Club ≠ SML team only
```

Future competitions should naturally appear under the club.

---

# 23. Engineering Content

Engineering content should answer:

```text
What problem?
What constraints?
What did we build?
Why did we choose this approach?
What happened?
What did we learn?
```

This creates credibility.

---

# 24. Project Writing

Every project should ideally include:

### Overview

What is it?

### Problem

What are we trying to solve?

### Approach

How did we approach it?

### Engineering

What technologies and systems were involved?

### Results

What actually happened?

### Lessons

What did we learn?

---

# 25. Do Not Force Every Section

If a project is simple, do not create:

```text
Problem
Methodology
Experimental Setup
Results
Discussion
Conclusion
```

just because it sounds academic.

Content length should reflect the project.

---

# 26. Technical Depth

Technical pages can be significantly more detailed than marketing pages.

For example:

```text
Homepage:
What we built.

Project:
How we built it.

Technical article:
Why we built it that way.
```

---

# 27. Team Member Bios

Member bios should feel human.

Avoid:

> Passionate robotics enthusiast with a deep commitment to innovation.

This is generic.

Prefer factual information:

> Works on autonomous navigation and perception, with a focus on ROS 2, SLAM, and LiDAR-based localization.

---

# 28. Member Bio Structure

Recommended:

```text
Role
Area(s)
Short bio
Skills
Projects
Education
Links
```

---

# 29. Roles

Roles should be flexible.

Examples:

```text
Team Lead
Mechanical Lead
Software Lead
Hardware Lead
Perception Engineer
Controls Engineer
Mechanical Engineer
Embedded Engineer
Research Member
Team Manager
```

These are examples, not a fixed enum.

---

# 30. Multiple Responsibilities

A member can have multiple responsibilities.

Example:

```text
Mechanical
+
Management
```

The UI should support this naturally.

---

# 31. Skills

Skills should be structured where possible.

Examples:

```text
ROS 2
C++
Python
YOLO
SLAM
LiDAR
SolidWorks
Altium Designer
PCB Design
Embedded Systems
```

---

# 32. Skills Should Not Become Badges Everywhere

Do not create 15 colorful badges underneath every member.

Use skills strategically.

Possible:

```text
ROS 2 · C++ · SLAM · LiDAR
```

rather than:

```text
[ROS2] [C++] [Python] [SLAM] [LiDAR] [YOLO] ...
```

---

# 33. Project Technology Display

Technology lists should be compact.

Example:

```text
ROS 2 · C++ · LiDAR · SLAM
```

rather than large icon grids.

---

# 34. Competition Content

Competition pages should prioritize factual information:

```text
Competition
Year
League
Team
Robot
Result
Awards
```

Then provide narrative content if available.

---

# 35. Results

Never exaggerate results.

If the team did not place:

Do not invent:

> Outstanding performance.

Instead:

> Completed our first competitive season and gained experience in autonomous industrial robotics.

---

# 36. Awards

Awards should be presented exactly as awarded.

Include:

```text
Award name
Competition
Year
```

and optional description.

---

# 37. News

News should be concise.

Examples:

> We completed the first prototype of our mobile platform.

> Our team has registered for the 2026 Smart Manufacturing League.

> Three new members joined the software division.

---

# 38. News Dates

Always store an actual publication date.

Do not manually write dates into article text when the system can display them automatically.

---

# 39. Blog

Blog posts should be more editorial and technical.

Possible topics:

```text
Building our first robot
Why we chose ROS 2
Designing our first PCB
Lessons from our first competition
SLAM experiments
YOLO on Jetson
Mechanical design decisions
```

---

# 40. Blog Authors

Every blog post should have an author.

This reinforces that the content comes from actual team members.

---

# 41. Multi-Author Articles

Support multiple authors where useful.

A technical project may have:

```text
Mechanical contributor
+
Software contributor
```

This is preferable to forcing one person to be the sole author.

---

# 42. Gallery Captions

Captions should add context.

Bad:

> Team photo

Better:

> Testing the mobile platform before the first SML qualification run.

---

# 43. Gallery Metadata

Where useful:

```text
Title
Caption
Date
Category
Project
Competition
People
```

This will become increasingly valuable as the club grows.

---

# 44. Sponsors

Sponsor descriptions should be factual.

Example:

> Company X supports the team with manufacturing equipment and technical resources.

Do not write:

> Company X is a world-leading revolutionary organization changing the future.

unless the sponsor specifically provides approved copy.

---

# 45. Sponsor Website Links

Every sponsor should have an optional external website URL.

The sponsor logo itself can be clickable.

The external link should open safely.

---

# 46. Sponsor Tiers

The admin should manage tiers.

The public website should not assume that tiers must be:

```text
Gold
Silver
Bronze
```

The organization may later choose:

```text
Strategic Partner
Technology Partner
Industry Partner
Supporter
```

---

# 47. Partner Content

Partners should be presented separately.

Example:

> We collaborate with organizations that contribute expertise, resources, or technical support to our work.

---

# 48. Join Page Tone

The Join page should be encouraging but selective.

Do not imply:

> Anyone can join instantly.

Instead:

> We are looking for students who want to build, learn, and contribute.

---

# 49. Beginner-Friendly Messaging

The team is student-oriented.

Therefore, do not make the website appear to require expert-level experience.

A useful message:

> You don't need to know everything before joining. You need the willingness to learn and contribute.

Again, this is a tone direction, not necessarily final copy.

---

# 50. Selection Process

If the team has a selection process, explain it.

Possible:

```text
Application
 ↓
Resume review
 ↓
Technical / team evaluation
 ↓
Interview
 ↓
Onboarding
```

Do not publish steps that are not actually used.

---

# 51. Contact Copy

Keep contact simple.

Example structure:

```text
Have a question, want to collaborate, or want to work with the team?

Get in touch.
```

Then:

```text
Email
Social links
Form
```

---

# 52. Form Success Messages

Use concise confirmation.

For example:

> Your message has been received. We'll get back to you as soon as we can.

Persian:

> پیام شما دریافت شد. در اولین فرصت با شما تماس خواهیم گرفت.

Do not promise a response time unless one exists.

---

# 53. Join Application Success

The success message should communicate that the application will be reviewed.

Example:

> Your application has been submitted. We'll review your information and get in touch if we need to continue the process.

Persian:

> درخواست شما با موفقیت ارسال شد. اطلاعات شما بررسی خواهد شد و در صورت نیاز برای ادامه فرایند با شما تماس خواهیم گرفت.

---

# 54. Persian Formality

Persian should be professional but natural.

Avoid overly bureaucratic language.

Bad:

> بدین‌وسیله به استحضار می‌رساند...

Prefer:

> خوشحالیم که به تیم ما علاقه‌مند هستید.

---

# 55. English Formality

English should be professional but not corporate.

Avoid:

> We hereby announce...

Prefer:

> We're building...

or:

> Our team is working on...

---

# 56. First-Person Voice

The website can use:

```text
we
our team
our robots
```

This makes the organization feel human.

---

# 57. Avoid Excessive "We"

Do not begin every paragraph with:

> We...

Mix:

- Statements.
- Technical descriptions.
- Facts.
- Quotes.
- Project narratives.

---

# 58. Content Components

Create reusable content components such as:

```text id="f7kwbe"
<RichText />
<Prose />
<ProjectSummary />
<SpecificationTable />
<TechStack />
<Timeline />
<Quote />
<Callout />
```

---

# 59. Rich Text

The blog/content editor should support structured content.

Do not store arbitrary HTML unless there is a strong reason.

---

# 60. Markdown vs Rich Text

The implementation can use either:

```text id="g0a5g0"
Markdown
```

or a structured rich-text format.

For a small student team, Markdown can be a reasonable starting point for technical articles.

However, the admin interface must make editing comfortable.

---

# 61. Admin Translation Workflow

For bilingual content:

```text id="4r1k17"
Create content
      ↓
English
      ↓
Persian
      ↓
Preview
      ↓
Publish
```

The admin should clearly see if the Persian translation is missing.

---

# 62. Translation Status

The admin editor can display:

```text id="6o3wme"
English: ✓
Persian: ✓
```

or:

```text id="pf6eq3"
English: ✓
Persian: —
```

---

# 63. Missing Translation Behavior

If Persian is missing:

- Prefer English fallback.
- Do not display blank sections.
- Do not generate fake translation.
- Make the missing translation obvious in admin.

---

# 64. Language Switch Persistence

The user's language choice should persist between visits through the cookie.

---

# 65. Language Default

Default to:

```text id="r8fgp9"
English
```

unless implementation requirements or browser/user preferences suggest otherwise.

---

# 66. Persian RTL

When Persian is active:

```text id="8lyp5d"
dir="rtl"
```

should apply globally.

The layout should be genuinely RTL.

---

# 67. RTL Technical Details

Check:

- Margins.
- Padding.
- Flex directions.
- Breadcrumbs.
- Arrows.
- Icons.
- Forms.
- Timeline.
- Cards.
- Tables.
- Navigation.
- Modal alignment.

Do not solve RTL by simply adding `text-right`.

---

# 68. Numbers

Technical numbers and measurements should remain readable.

Examples:

```text id="x5ij8w"
2026
12 members
2.5 kg
10 m
```

Use appropriate direction isolation when mixing Persian text and Latin technical content.

---

# 69. URLs and Code

URLs, code, GitHub repositories, package names, and technical identifiers should remain LTR.

---

# 70. Date Formatting

The website should support localized date presentation.

English can use:

```text id="y5gxxu"
August 12, 2026
```

Persian can use an appropriate Persian presentation.

If the site later adopts the Persian calendar, implement it consistently rather than mixing calendars randomly.

---

# 71. Content SEO

Every indexable content item should have:

```text id="z8n3m4"
SEO title
SEO description
```

with sensible defaults from its title/content.

---

# 72. SEO Fields

Admin should optionally be able to override:

```text id="kq8zsh"
SEO title EN
SEO description EN
SEO title FA
SEO description FA
```

If empty, generate reasonable defaults.

---

# 73. Slug Editing

Admins should be able to define a slug.

Once published, changing a slug should be treated carefully.

Ideally, the system should eventually support redirects from old slugs.

---

# 74. Internal Linking

Articles should be able to link to:

```text id="cnxwgl"
Projects
Robots
Members
Competitions
```

This improves navigation and SEO.

---

# 75. Related Content

At the end of pages, show relevant content.

Example:

```text id="4q4l1x"
Related projects
Related articles
Related competitions
```

Do not simply show random content.

---

# 76. Content Recommendations

Recommendations should preferably be based on:

```text id="jczb2n"
same project
same robot
same competition
same technology
same category
```

rather than arbitrary ordering.

---

# 77. Homepage Content Management

The admin should control major homepage sections.

However, do not turn the homepage into a fully generic page builder.

The visual design should remain controlled.

---

# 78. Admin-Controlled Homepage

Allow configuration of:

```text id="n3l9g0"
Featured project
Featured robot
Featured competition
Featured members
Featured articles
Sponsors
```

rather than allowing administrators to arbitrarily rearrange every pixel.

---

# 79. Why Not a Page Builder

A generic drag-and-drop page builder would:

- Increase complexity.
- Reduce visual consistency.
- Make SEO harder.
- Make the design easier to break.
- Create maintenance problems.

The website should be a **designed system**, not a blank CMS canvas.

---

# 80. Editorial Control

The admin should control content.

The frontend controls presentation.

This separation is essential.

---

# 81. Content Hierarchy

Every piece of content should have a clear hierarchy:

```text
Title
 ↓
Summary
 ↓
Main content
 ↓
Supporting information
 ↓
Related content
```

---

# 82. Content Length

Do not force everything to be short.

Use:

```text
Homepage → concise
About → medium
Project → medium/deep
Technical article → deep
Member → concise
News → short
```

---

# 83. Real Photos Over Decorative Content

When real team photos become available, they should replace generic placeholders.

The website should gradually become more visual through actual documentation.

---

# 84. First-Year Storytelling

The first year itself is valuable content.

Potential story:

```text
Starting the club
 ↓
Building the first robot
 ↓
Learning the stack
 ↓
Testing
 ↓
Entering SML
 ↓
Competition
 ↓
Lessons learned
```

This will become the foundation of the club's history.

---

# 85. Documentation Mindset

Treat the website partly as the club's public engineering archive.

Over time, visitors should be able to look back and see:

```text
What we built
Why we built it
Who built it
What happened
What we learned
```

---

# 86. Long-Term Content Value

A project published in 2026 should still make sense when someone visits in 2030.

Therefore include:

```text
Year
Competition
Team members
Technologies
Version/context
```

where relevant.

---

# 87. Alumni Value

As people leave the team, their contribution should remain visible.

A former member's profile can show:

```text
Alumni
Joined 2026
Worked on X
Worked on Y
```

This creates institutional memory.

---

# 88. Avoid Vanity Metrics

Do not add:

```text
10,000+ hours
500+ commits
999% dedication
```

unless they are real and useful.

---

# 89. Statistics Must Be Defensible

Every displayed statistic should have a clear source.

Prefer database-derived statistics.

---

# 90. Final Content Principle

The website's strongest content will not be slogans.

It will be:

```text
Real robot
+
Real project
+
Real person
+
Real engineering problem
+
Real result
```

That combination should be the foundation of the entire content strategy.
