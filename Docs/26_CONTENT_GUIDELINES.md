# Content Guidelines Specification

## 1. Purpose

This document defines how content for the robotics club website should be written, structured, reviewed, translated, and presented.

The website represents a **robotics club at IUST** whose current competitive focus is the **Smart Manufacturing League (SML)**, while positioning the organization as a broader robotics club capable of expanding into additional competitions, research, engineering projects, and educational activities.

The content must communicate:

> **We are an engineering-focused robotics club that builds real systems, competes seriously, learns together, and grows as a team.**

The website must **not** make the club appear larger, older, or more accomplished than it actually is.

---

# 2. Core Content Principles

Every piece of content should follow these principles:

1. **Technical credibility**
2. **Honesty**
3. **Clarity**
4. **Professionalism**
5. **Human character**
6. **Engineering substance**
7. **Student energy**
8. **Future potential**

---

# 3. Positioning

The club should be presented as:

```text
Robotics Club
    ↓
Engineering
    ↓
Competition
    ↓
Education
    ↓
Research
    ↓
Community
```

SML is the **current competitive focus**, not the definition of the entire organization.

---

# 4. Current Identity

Until the robotics club has an official name and logo:

- Do not invent a permanent team name.
- Do not create a fake acronym.
- Do not create a final logo.
- Do not hardcode the identity into the architecture.

The website should use a temporary neutral identity where necessary.

Example:

> IUST Robotics Club

This may later be replaced without redesigning the website.

---

# 5. IUST Relationship

The club is associated with:

**Iran University of Science and Technology (IUST)**

The website may communicate this relationship clearly.

However, content must **not imply official university sponsorship, endorsement, funding, or institutional ownership unless that relationship is formally confirmed.**

Prefer wording such as:

> A robotics club at Iran University of Science and Technology.

rather than:

> The official robotics club of IUST.

unless that statement is actually true.

---

# 6. Current Competition

The club currently participates in:

**Smart Manufacturing League (SML)**

SML should be described as a competition within the industrial/industrial-robotics direction of RoboCup where appropriate.

Do not repeatedly describe the entire club as:

> an SML team.

Instead:

> Our current competitive work is focused on the Smart Manufacturing League.

---

# 7. Future Expansion

The content architecture must leave room for future competitions.

The website should be able to communicate:

> Our current work begins with SML, but our interests extend across robotics, automation, AI, embedded systems, and intelligent machines.

Do not promise participation in competitions that have not been confirmed.

---

# 8. Voice

The preferred voice is:

**70% professional + 30% student**

This means:

### Professional

- Precise
- Confident
- Technical
- Structured
- Mature
- Evidence-based

### Student

- Curious
- Energetic
- Human
- Ambitious
- Approachable

---

# 9. Avoid Corporate Language

Do not use excessive corporate language such as:

> We leverage cutting-edge synergies to revolutionize the robotics ecosystem.

Instead:

> We design and build robotic systems for real competition environments.

---

# 10. Avoid Startup Language

Do not present the club like a venture-backed startup.

Avoid:

> We're disrupting the future of industrial robotics.

Prefer:

> We are a student-led robotics club focused on building, testing, and improving autonomous robotic systems.

---

# 11. Avoid Artificial Grandiosity

The club is in its first year.

Do not use language suggesting decades of experience.

Avoid:

> Decades of robotics excellence.

Prefer:

> Our first year is the beginning of a longer engineering journey.

---

# 12. First-Year Positioning

Being new should not be treated as a weakness.

The content should frame the first year around:

- Building foundations.
- Learning.
- Experimentation.
- Team formation.
- First competition.
- Establishing engineering processes.
- Creating a long-term club.

Example:

> This is our first year as a team. We are using it to build the technical foundation, engineering culture, and teamwork that will shape the years ahead.

---

# 13. Homepage Messaging

The homepage should answer three questions quickly:

### Who are we?

A robotics club at IUST.

### What do we do?

Build robotic systems through engineering, competition, education, and research.

### Why should I care?

Because visitors can follow what we build, meet the people behind it, and potentially become part of it.

# 25. Projects

Projects should explain what the team actually built.

A project page should ideally answer:

1. What was it?
2. Why did we build it?
3. What problem did it solve?
4. How did we build it?
5. What technologies were used?
6. Who worked on it?
7. What did we learn?
8. What happened next?

---

# 26. Project Writing

Bad:

> A revolutionary autonomous robot using advanced AI.

Better:

> An autonomous mobile platform combining LiDAR-based mapping, SLAM, and vision-based object detection.

# 38. Engineering Honesty

Technical content should acknowledge limitations.

Example:

> The first version performed reliably indoors but struggled with reflective surfaces, which led us to redesign the sensor placement.

This is better than pretending every prototype worked perfectly.

---

Failures and lessons learned can be valuable content.

- Correct terminology.
- Proper punctuation.
- Proper RTL structure.

---

# 67. Translation Principle

Translate **meaning**, not sentence structure.

For example:

English:

> Building robots. Learning by building.

should not necessarily become a literal word-for-word Persian sentence.

The Persian version should sound natural.

# 83. No AI-Generated Feel

This is a major requirement.

Content must avoid the common characteristics of AI-generated websites:

- Generic inspirational slogans.
- Excessive gradients.
- Meaningless buzzwords.
- Repeated phrases.
- Overly polished fake testimonials.
- Generic "future of innovation" language.
- Excessive statistics.
- Fake quotes.
- Fake achievements.

---

# 84. Human Writing

Whenever possible, content should include concrete details that could only come from the team.

Examples:

> We spent three evenings debugging the LiDAR mounting angle before realizing the vibration was affecting the scan.

This feels more authentic than:

> Through countless iterations, our team pushed the boundaries of innovation.

---

# 85. Testimonials

Do not create fake testimonials.

If testimonials are added later, they must come from real people with permission.

---

# 86. Quotes

Quotes from members should be:

- Real.
- Approved.
- Attributed.

Do not invent motivational quotes for team members.

unless the team has actually measured and documented that result.

when accurate.

# 159. Final Editorial Rule

# 160. Final Content Philosophy

The website should not try to convince visitors that the team is already world-famous.

It should make visitors want to **follow the journey**.

The strongest narrative for the first year is:

> **We are at the beginning. We are building the team, building the robots, learning from the failures, competing in SML, and creating the foundation for what comes next.**

That is more credible, more memorable, and more compelling than pretending the club has already reached its destination.
